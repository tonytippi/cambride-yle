## 1. Overview of Cambridge English Qualifications

Cambridge English Qualifications are in-depth exams that make learning English enjoyable, effective, and rewarding [4]. They encourage continuous progression with a clear path to improving language skills [4]. Each qualification focuses on a level of the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR), enabling learners to develop and build speaking, writing, reading, and listening skills [4].

### Key Statistics:
* **5.9 million** assessments taken every year [4].
* Accepted by over **25,000 organisations** worldwide [4].
* **2,800 exam centres** in 130 countries [4].
* Over **50,000 preparation centres** [4].

### CEFR Levels & Scale Scores:
The exams provide a reliable and consistent measure of how well a child is doing [6]. There is no pass or fail; all children receive a certificate showing how many shields (out of five) they receive in each skill [9].

| Exam Level | Target CEFR Level | Cambridge English Scale Scores | Typical Ability Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre A1 Starters** | Pre A1 [8] | Below 100 [10] | Can understand simple spoken questions about self; can copy words, phrases, and spell very simple words [23, 24]. |
| **A1 Movers** | Pre A1 and A1 [8] | 100–119 [11] | Can understand simple signs; write short phrases/sentences about pictures; ask simple questions about habits [25, 26]. |
| **A2 Flyers** | A1 and A2 [8] | 120–139 [10] | Can understand simple written/spoken descriptions; write short, simple stories; ask basic questions on everyday topics [27, 28]. |

## 3. Pre A1 Starters: Detailed Papers & Tasks

### Listening Paper (About 20 mins, 20 items) [22, 33]:
* **Part 1 (5 questions):** Matching. Draw lines to match names to people in a picture [33].
* **Part 2 (5 questions):** Note-taking. Write numbers (1–20 dictated) and names (spelled out letter-by-letter) in gaps [33, 40].
* **Part 3 (5 questions):** 3-option multiple choice. Tick the correct box (A, B, or C) under pictures [33, 42].
* **Part 4 (5 questions):** Colouring. Carry out instructions, locate objects, and colour correctly [33].

### Reading and Writing Paper (20 mins, 25 items) [22, 45, 46]:
* **Part 1 (5 questions):** True/false vocabulary recognition. Tick (✔) or cross (✘) to show if a sentence matches a picture [45, 51].
* **Part 2 (5 questions):** Reading comprehension based on a picture. Write "yes" or "no" to show if a statement is correct [46, 53].
* **Part 3 (5 questions):** Spelling. Write words by unscrambling jumbled letters under pictures of objects [46, 55, 56].
* **Part 4 (5 questions):** Multiple-choice cloze. Choose and copy missing words from a box into a short factual or semi-factual text [46, 59].
* **Part 5 (5 questions):** Answer questions based on a picture story. Write one-word answers to questions about a story told through three pictures [46, 61].

### Speaking Paper (3–5 mins, 4 parts) [22, 63]:
* **Part 1:** Interlocutor-candidate interaction using a scene picture and object cards. Candidate points to correct parts of the picture and places object cards as directed [63].
* **Part 2:** Interlocutor-candidate interaction using the scene picture. Candidate answers simple questions with short answers (including a "Tell me about..." question) [63].
* **Part 3:** Interlocutor-candidate interaction using object cards. Candidate answers simple questions about four object cards [63, 74].
* **Part 4:** Interlocutor-candidate interaction. Personal questions on topics such as age, family, school, and friends [63, 75].

## 4. A1 Movers: Detailed Papers & Tasks

### Listening Paper (About 25 mins, 25 items) [22, 85]:
* **Part 1 (5 questions):** Matching. Draw lines to match names to people in a picture [85].
* **Part 2 (5 questions):** Note-taking. Write words or numbers in gaps on a form or notepad page [85, 91].
* **Part 3 (5 questions):** Matching. Match a list of illustrated words or names with a set of pictures by writing letters (A–H) in boxes [85, 93].
* **Part 4 (5 questions):** 3-option multiple choice. Tick the correct box under pictures [85].
* **Part 5 (5 questions):** Colouring and writing. Carry out instructions to colour objects and write a simple, logical word in the space indicated [85, 97, 98].

### Reading and Writing Paper (30 mins, 39 marks) [101]:
* **Part 1 (5 questions):** Matching words to definitions. Read short definitions and match/copy the correct words next to them (supported by labelled pictures) [99, 106].
* **Part 2 (6 questions):** Short dialogue with multiple-choice responses. Complete a dialogue by choosing from three options (A, B, or C) for each turn [100, 108].
* **Part 3 (6 questions):** Gapped text. Choose the correct words from a box to complete a short narrative, and choose the best title from a choice of three [100, 112].
* **Part 4 (5 questions):** Multiple-choice cloze. Choose the correct word from three grammatical options to complete each gap in a factual text [100, 116].
* **Part 5 (7 questions):** Sentence completion based on a story. Complete sentences about a story using one, two, or three words taken directly from the text [101, 119].
* **Part 6 (6 questions):** Productive writing. Complete sentences (Questions 1-2), answer questions (Questions 3-4), and write two sentences about a picture (Questions 5-6) [101, 122, 123].

### Speaking Paper (5–7 mins, 4 parts) [22, 128]:
* **Part 1:** Identify four differences between two similar-looking pictures (e.g., colour, size, number, position) [128, 131].
* **Part 2:** Describe each picture in turn from a four-picture sequence that shows a story [128, 132].
* **Part 3:** Identify the "odd one out" in four sets of pictures and give a simple reason for the choice [128, 135].
* **Part 4:** Answer simple open-ended personal questions about school, weekends, friends, hobbies, etc. [128, 136].

## 5. A2 Flyers: Detailed Papers & Tasks

### Listening Paper (About 25 mins, 25 items) [22, 146]:
* **Part 1 (5 questions):** Matching. Draw lines to match names to people in a picture [146].
* **Part 2 (5 questions):** Note-taking. Write words or numbers in gaps on a form or notepad [146, 154].
* **Part 3 (5 questions):** Matching. Match a list of illustrated words or names with a set of pictures by writing letters in boxes [146, 156].
* **Part 4 (5 questions):** 3-option multiple choice. Tick the correct box under pictures [147].
* **Part 5 (5 questions):** Colouring and writing. Carry out instructions to colour objects and write two simple words [147, 159].

### Reading and Writing Paper (40 mins, 48 marks) [22, 163]:
* **Part 1 (10 questions):** Matching words to definitions. Choose and copy the correct word (from 15 unillustrated options) next to 10 definitions [161, 169].
* **Part 2 (5 questions):** Continuous dialogue. Complete a dialogue by choosing the correct response from a list of options (A–H) [162, 173].
* **Part 3 (6 questions):** Gapped text. Choose the correct word from a box to complete a short narrative, and choose the best title from a choice of three [162, 177].
* **Part 4 (10 questions):** Multiple-choice cloze. Choose the correct word from three grammatical options to complete each gap in a factual text [162, 181].
* **Part 5 (7 questions):** Sentence completion based on a story. Complete sentences about a story using one, two, three, or four words [163, 185].
* **Part 6 (5 questions):** Open cloze. Read a short text (diary/email) and complete the gaps with your own words (no options given) [163, 189].
* **Part 7 (1 question):** Productive writing. Write a short story of 20 or more words based on three pictures [163, 191, 192].

### Speaking Paper (7–9 mins, 4 parts) [22, 200, 201]:
* **Part 1:** Identify six differences between the candidate’s picture and the examiner’s picture in response to oral statements made by the examiner [200, 204].
* **Part 2:** Answer and ask questions about two people, objects, or situations using a set of information and question cues [201, 207].
* **Part 3:** Describe each picture in turn from a five-picture sequence that shows a story [201, 209].
* **Part 4:** Answer personal open-ended questions about school, holidays, birthdays, family, and hobbies [201, 212].

## 6. Grammar and Structures List

### Pre A1 Starters Level [30, 31, 32]:
* **Nouns:** Singular and plural (including irregular forms, countable/uncountable, names). *Example: Nora is my friend.* [30]
* **Adjectives:** Including possessive adjectives. *Example: His name is Yusef.* [30]
* **Determiners:** *Example: This is an apple. I want some milk.* [30]
* **Pronouns:** Demonstrative, personal, possessive, interrogative, and "one". *Example: This is mine!* [30]
* **Verbs:** Contractions, positive, negative, questions, imperatives, and short answers in:
  * Present simple (*Nick is happy. I don't like eggs.*) [31]
  * Present continuous (not with future reference) (*The cat's sleeping.*) [31]
  * "Can" for ability or requests (*Can I have some birthday cake?*) [31]
  * "Have (got)" for possession (*She hasn't got a dog.*) [31]
* **Adverbs:** *Example: I'm colouring it now. My grandma lives here.* [31]
* **Conjunctions:** *Example: I've got a pen and a pencil.* [32]
* **Prepositions of place and time:** *Example: Put the clock next to the picture. We go to school in the morning.* [32]
* **Question words:** *Who, Where, etc.* [32]
* **Impersonal "you":** *How do you spell that?* [32]
* **Have + object + infinitive:** *Lucy has a book to read.* [32]
* **-ing forms as nouns:** *Swimming is good.* [32]
* **Key Phrases:** *Let's...*, *Like + v + ing*, *There is/There are*, *Would like + n/v*, *Happy birthday!*, *Here you are*, *Me too!*, *So do I*, *Story about + -ing*, *What (a/an) + adj + n*, *What now?* [79, 80].

### A1 Movers Level (In addition to Starters level) [81, 137]:
* **Indirect objects:** *Give it to the teacher!* [81, 138]
* **Comparative and superlative adjectives:** *Your house is bigger than mine. Anna is my best friend.* [81, 138]
* **Verbs:**
  * Past simple regular and irregular forms (*We went to the park yesterday. Her father cooked lunch.*) [82, 138]
  * Verb + infinitive (*I want to go home. He started to laugh.*) [82, 138]
  * Verb + -ing (*I went riding on Saturday.*) [82, 138]
  * Infinitive of purpose (*She went to town to buy a toothbrush.*) [82, 139]
  * Want/ask someone to do something (*He wants the teacher to tell a story.*) [82, 139]
  * "Must" for obligation/prohibition (*He must do his homework. You mustn't give the rabbit cheese.*) [82, 139]
  * "Have (got) to/had to" (*I've got to go. He had to draw a whale.*) [83, 139]
  * "Shall" for offers (*Shall I help you wash the car, Mum?*) [83, 139]
  * "Could" (past form of can) (*I could see some birds.*) [83, 139]
* **Adverbs:** *She never eats meat. He sang loudly. My brother reads more quickly than my sister.* [83, 139, 140]
* **Conjunctions:** *I went home because I was tired.* [83, 140]
* **Prepositions of time:** *She plays with her friends after school.* [83, 140]
* **Question words:** *Why is he talking to her? When does school start?* [84, 140]
* **Relative clauses:** *Vicky is the girl who is riding a bike. This is the house where my friend lives.* [84, 140]
* **Key Phrases:** *What is/was the weather like?*, *What's the matter?*, *How/What about + n or -ing*, *When clauses (not with future meaning)*, *Go for a + n*, *Be called + n*, *Be good at + n*, *I think/know...* [84, 141].

### A2 Flyers Level (In addition to Starters and Movers levels) [142, 213]:
* **Verbs:**
  * Past continuous for interrupted actions/background setting (*I was walking down the road when I saw her.*) [143, 214]
  * Present perfect (*Have you ever been to the circus? He's just eaten his dinner.*) [143, 214]
  * "Be going to" (*It isn't going to rain today.*) [143, 214]
  * "Will" for predictions/intentions (*Will you do your homework this evening?*) [143, 214]
  * "Might" (*Vicky might come to the party.*) [143, 214]
  * "May" (*The bus may not come today.*) [144, 215]
  * "Shall" for suggestions (*Shall we have a picnic in the park?*) [144, 215]
  * "Could" and "Should" (*You could invite Robert. Should we take a towel?*) [144, 215]
  * Tag questions (*That's John's book, isn't it?*) [144, 215]
* **Adverbs:** *I haven't bought my brother's birthday present yet.* [144, 215]
* **Conjunctions:** *I didn't want to walk home so I went on the bus.* [144, 215]
* **Conditional Clauses:** *If clauses in zero conditionals (If it's sunny, we go swimming).* [144, 215]
* **Relative / Subordinate Clauses:** *Where clauses (My grandmother has forgotten where she put her glasses)*; *Before/after clauses (I finished my homework before I played football).* [144, 145, 215, 216]
* **Descriptive Phrases:** *Be/look/sound/feel/taste/smell like (What's your new teacher like?)*; *Make somebody/something + adj (That smell makes me hungry!)*; *Be made of (The toy is made of wood).* [145, 216]
* **Key Phrases:** *What time...?*, *What else/next?*, *See you soon/later/tomorrow etc.* [145, 216].

## 8. Speaking Assessment Scales

Speaking Examiners (SEs) assess candidates throughout the test using criteria defined in terms of candidate behaviour [231].

### Pre A1 Starters Scale Criteria (0 to 5 points) [231]:
1. **Vocabulary:** Range and control of words required to deal with tasks [232].
2. **Pronunciation:** Extent of individual sounds and word stress intelligibility [232].
3. **Interaction:** Reception, responding, support required, and fluency/promptness [232].

### A1 Movers Scale Criteria (0 to 5 points) [235]:
1. **Vocabulary and Grammar:** Range and control of vocabulary and simple structures [235].
2. **Pronunciation:** Intelligibility, control of word stress, and intonation [235, 236].
3. **Interaction:** Appropriacy of response, support required (very little expected at band 5), and promptness [235, 236].

### A2 Flyers Scale Criteria (0 to 5 points) [239]:
1. **Vocabulary and Grammar:** Range of vocabulary and range of simple structures utilized [240].
2. **Pronunciation:** Intelligibility, control of stress, and intonation at both word and longer utterance levels [240].
3. **Interaction:** Appropriate response to all instructions/questions, minimal support needed, prompt response with natural hesitation [240, 241].

### Glossary of Speaking Scale Terms [244]:
* **Range:** Degree to which candidates have vocabulary to deal with the tasks [244].
* **Control:** Degree to which candidates can use simple structures in the syllabus. Complete accuracy is not expected, but errors must not obscure meaning [244].
* **Extent:** Length of the candidate's utterances (ranges from single-word responses at Starters to slightly longer sentences at Flyers) [245].
* **Cohesion:** Ability to join ideas using simple linkers (*and, but, then, because*) [245].
* **Individual sounds:** Pronunciation of vowels, diphthongs, and consonants [245].
* **Stress:** Emphasis laid on a syllable or word [246].
* **Intonation:** Voice rising/falling to convey mood or support meaning [246].
* **Reception/Responding:** Replying or reacting to the examiner [247].
* **Support required:** Level of prompting needed from the examiner [247].
* **Fluency/Promptness:** Fluidity of expression without excessive halting [247].
